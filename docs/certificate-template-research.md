# Quiz participation certificate template

Research date: 28 September 2026.

Hope Cards uses an original participation-certificate layout adapted from the institutional examples below. These examples establish common wording and document structure; they do not establish a universal officially approved template or confer institutional accreditation on certificates generated by the app.

## Template and language decisions

- Use A4 landscape, a restrained border, generous margins, and Hope Cards typography and colors.
- Place the organization, when provided, at the top, followed by the participation title, a prominent participant name, an explicit participation statement, the competition title, and the date.
- Provide a blank line for the organizer's signature at the bottom. Do not reproduce institutional logos, seals, signatures, or claims of accreditation.
- Certify participation only. Do not imply a score, placement, qualification, or successful examination.
- Offer any supported Bible language, including English. Translate the certificate's fixed wording and untouched default fields. Preserve custom participant names, competition titles, and organization text when the language changes. Select the date through a date picker and display it in the certificate's output language.
- Keep installation invitations and the Play Store link in the accompanying share message, outside the PDF.

Malayalam needs its own sentence order rather than a literal translation of English “presented to / for participation in” fragments. The implemented wording is an original, gender-neutral adaptation:

```text
[ORGANIZATION, IF PROVIDED]

പങ്കാളിത്ത സാക്ഷ്യപത്രം

[PARTICIPANT NAME]

താഴെപ്പറയുന്ന മത്സരത്തിൽ പങ്കെടുത്തതായി സാക്ഷ്യപ്പെടുത്തുന്നു:

[COMPETITION TITLE]

തീയതി: [DATE]

____________________
സംഘാടകരുടെ ഒപ്പ്
```

The longer heading “പങ്കാളിത്ത സാക്ഷ്യപത്രം” is our adaptation of the institutional heading “സാക്ഷ്യപത്രം”. The attestation refers forward to the competition, so arbitrary names and titles do not need grammatical suffixes. Malayalam uses Noto Sans Malayalam. The Filipino title is “Katibayan ng Paglahok”. Other localized sentences are adaptations for a quiz competition, not verbatim institutional templates.
